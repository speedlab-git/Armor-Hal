ARMOR-HAL

A unified defense for adversarial robustness and hallucination mitigation in large vision-language models.

Today's LVLM defenses treat jailbreaks and hallucinations as separate problems, and fixing one routinely worsens the other. ARMOR-HAL starts from a simple observation: the two failures live at different moments of decoding. Building on this observation, ARMOR-HAL applies a targeted intervention at the decoding stage associated with each failure mode, enabling it to mitigate both jailbreaks and hallucinations at inference time without retraining the underlying model.

01

Why one defense has never been enough

The project set out to remove a trade-off that existing LVLM defenses accept as given.

Jailbreak exploits take hold in the first few output tokens. Hallucinations surface in mid- and late-phase decoding. A defense that respects that timing doesn’t have to trade one for the other.
JB

Shallow safety alignment

Token-level alignment leaves LVLMs open to adversarial images, suffix manipulations and universal perturbations. Adversarial training helps, at a high per-model cost.

HL

Robustness that costs grounding

In our HALS study (NeurIPS 2026), we found that LVLMs with adversarially robust vision encoders hallucinate more frequently than those with standard encoders. We trace this increase to visual token homogenization.

?!

Knowing when the model is wrong

A deployed defense also needs a cheap signal of failure. Trajectory-Lens (EMNLP 2026) reads it from the model’s own forward pass, in 4.6 ms.

02

Two thrusts, one inference-time pipeline

Both thrusts precompute small steering directions once, then apply them only when and where they are needed. No model weights change.

Thrust I · early tokens

Adaptive activation steering for jailbreak robustness

A phase-aware safety firewall. ARMOR-HAL contrasts hidden states on benign and adversarially perturbed inputs to extract lightweight harmful directions, then watches for them during the first tokens of a response.

  1. Calibrate once. Derive harmful activation directions from a small calibration set; reuse them across prompts.
  2. Gate by projection. At inference, project activations onto the harmful direction. Below the threshold, do nothing, so benign prompts stay untouched.
  3. Steer early. Above it, remove the harmful component, mainly in the first output tokens where adversarial intent takes hold.

Thrust II · mid & late tokens

Cross-modal stabilization for hallucination mitigation

Safety mechanisms tend to weaken grounding. Thrust II anchors later tokens to the evidence with two stabilization directions, and scales them with how much the model is still looking at the image.

  1. Visual stability direction. Average encoder features across perturbed views of the same image to keep the signal that survives.
  2. Textual anti-hallucination direction. Contrast grounded and hallucinated generations to isolate the latent shift tied to ungrounded content.
  3. Adaptive scheduling. Raise the coefficient when attention to vision drops or attention-sink tokens dominate; ease off when grounding is healthy, so answers stay informative.

This thrust became HALS: homogenization-aware latent steering that restores grounding in robust LVLMs without touching the robust encoder.

Md Zarif Hossain, Awal Ahmed Fime, Ahmed Imteaj

Adversarially robust vision encoders raise inter-token similarity by up to 38%, so the decoder falls back on language priors. Homogenization-Aware Latent Steering (HALS) steers decoder hidden states back toward grounded output, training-free and with the robust encoder untouched.
